Duplicate Payment Testing with AI: Every AP Invoice, Plus the Three-Way Match

Test every AP invoice for duplicate payments and three-way match exceptions with AI, without uploading the ledger. Prompts, checks and limits.

Duplicate payment testing is one of the oldest tests in internal audit, and one of the easiest to do badly. An exact match on vendor, invoice number and amount finds the obvious pairs. It misses the invoice keyed as A0071355 one week and A71355 the next, and the same invoice paid again under a second vendor record that shares the first one's bank account. The three-way match has traps of its own: a PO line received in three lots looks like a short receipt to a careless join, and a price 1.5% over the PO may be inside tolerance.

An AI tool can write and run these tests on the full AP population, not a sample. Two questions come first: what leaves your laptop, and how do you know the answer is right?

The rule: let the AI write the script, not read the data

Claude Code is Anthropic's AI tool that works on files on your own computer. It needs a paid Claude plan, and for company data your organization should approve the tool and the plan first.

The model that does the thinking runs on Anthropic's servers, so the rule is short: whatever Claude Code reads, it sends. If it opens your AP register, the rows go to the provider. If it writes a Python script that reads the register on your laptop and prints only counts and totals, then only the headers, the script and the counts are sent. The detail stays in a workbook on your disk.

This is a working habit, not a security boundary: printed rows and some error messages are sent too, and an approved script runs with your access. Work in a folder holding only the copies this job needs, use Manual mode, and read each permission prompt.

What you need

  • ap_invoices.xlsx: the invoice register, with vendor, invoice number, dates, amount, PO number, status
  • po_lines.xlsx and goods_receipts.xlsx: PO lines and the receipts against them
  • vendor_master.xlsx: vendor ID, bank account, tax ID, created date
  • ap_policy_extract.txt: price tolerance and the categories allowed without a PO
  • ap_control_total.txt: the GL document count and net amount for the period

Look inside the book

Step 1: Learn the files without reading the data

Structure alone tells you a lot. In the book's tested run on practice data, this step noticed that the register was pulled by invoice date but the GL total by posting date, that some PO lines had several receipts, and that two vendor records shared a tax ID.

Step 2: Tie the register to the general ledger

Every test after this depends on the population being complete.

A difference is often a cut-off item, such as an invoice dated in June and posted in July. A tie on net totals cannot rule out missing items that offset; if you will rely on the population, ask for the GL document list.

Step 3: Run four duplicate tests

The normalization rule is a test-design decision; decide it and record it. The same-amount test will flag recurring charges such as monthly rent: explain those rows, don't report them. And a duplicate reversed by a credit memo before payment is the control working, not an exception.

Step 4: Test the three-way match

Be precise about wording. "Billed above received for that PO line" can be read per invoice or for all invoices on the line together. The book's two runs of the same prompt read it both ways and got different row counts; neither was wrong. Agree the definition and write it in the working paper.

The amount on each exception sheet is usually the whole invoice value. Report the exposure instead.

How to check the result

Fluent output is not evidence. These checks are:

  1. Control totals. Sum the register's amount column in Excel yourself and tie it to the GL figure, explaining the difference. Rows read should equal rows tested plus rows set aside (credit memos, negative amounts), and the sheets should add back.
  2. Re-perform an exception. Filter the register for one near-duplicate pair and confirm both invoices, the PO line and both payment dates. Check the shared bank account in the vendor master yourself.
  3. Re-perform a pass. Pick a PO line with several receipts that was not flagged and confirm the receipts add up to the quantity billed. Checking only what was found never catches what was wrongly left out.
  4. Re-run and compare.

An identical re-run shows the script is consistent. It does not show it is correct; the hand checks do that.

  1. Ask for assumptions. "List every assumption you made and everything you did not check." Which date, which price version, which currency: a script makes these choices silently.

File the script, the prompts, both workbooks and your re-performed items with a short note on population, tolerance, definitions and reviewer. That is what lets a reviewer, or next year's team, re-perform the work.

Limits

  • A flag is not a finding. Vouch each one to the invoice copies, the receipt, the bank payment and, for a second vendor record, the vendor creation request. Words like fraud belong to a different engagement.
  • The script tests only what is in the data. Approvals held outside the ERP, such as sign-off by legal on legal invoices, are not tested.
  • A script can be precisely and consistently wrong. Read its rules in plain English against your test design.
  • The script pattern reduces what is sent to the AI provider; it does not remove the need for approval, a clean working folder and careful permission prompts.
  • The extract is only as good as how it was obtained. Record the report, parameters and who ran it.
